# Olympic Lifting Hybrid Program — 8 Week Block

## Athlete profile

- Front squat e1RM: **~103 kg**
- Back squat e1RM: **~110 kg**
- Deadlift capacity: **~110 kg** (rarely trained)
- Barbell RDL capacity: **~100 kg**
- Strict press e1RM: **~57 kg**
- Bench press e1RM: **~82 kg**
- Comfortable clean: **~50 kg**
- Current ugly clean max: **~55 kg**
- Historical snatch: **~40 kg**
- Training availability: **2–3 gym sessions/week**
- Current BJJ: **none**
- Mobility limitations: **none notable**
- Gym: rotating Olympic bars, bumper plates, platform, rack, overhead clearance
- Constraint: **no intentional bar slamming / dropping**

## Main diagnosis

Your strength base is ahead of your Olympic-lifting skill. The short-term bottleneck is therefore not raw strength but bar path, timing, speed under the bar, receiving mechanics, and jerk footwork.

The first target is not simply **clean 60 kg**. It is:

> **Make 50 kg look boring.**

## Technique scoring

Score each Olympic-lift rep:

- **3 — Clean:** balanced, fast, repeatable.
- **2 — Acceptable:** small error but fundamentally sound.
- **1 — Ugly:** meaningful technical breakdown.
- **0 — Miss.**

**Rule:** two consecutive score-1 reps = reduce the load by **5–10%**.

Track **technical maxes** separately from absolute maxes. A technical max is the heaviest rep that is still good enough to reinforce the movement you actually want to own.

## No-drop constraint

Controlled lowering adds fatigue that competitive weightlifters normally avoid by dropping bumper-loaded bars. Therefore:

- keep snatch, clean, and jerk mostly submaximal;
- use pulls and squats for heavier loading;
- do not max the jerk if getting the bar down becomes awkward or unsafe;
- guide cleans from front rack to thighs, absorb with hips/knees, then lower;
- guide snatches from overhead to hip level, absorb, then lower;
- do not use normal plyo boxes as lifting blocks unless designed for bar impacts.

# Weekly program

## Day A — Snatch + Front Squat

**Snatch warm-up complex — 2–3 rounds**
- snatch-grip RDL × 5
- muscle snatch × 5
- overhead squat × 5
- snatch balance × 3
- hang power snatch × 3

**Hang Power Snatch — 5×2 @ 25–30 kg**  
Progress toward 30–35 kg only while crisp.

**Full Snatch — 6×1 @ ~30–35 kg**  
Repeatable singles. Avoid routine maxing.

**Snatch Pull — 4×3 @ 45–50 kg**  
Progress gradually toward 50–60+ kg.

**Front Squat — 4×3 @ 70–75 kg**  
Add ~2.5 kg when bar speed remains good. No grinders.

**Pull-ups — 3×5–8**

## Day B — Clean + Split Jerk

**Clean warm-up complex — 2–3 rounds**
- clean RDL × 5
- clean high pull × 3
- muscle clean × 3
- front squat × 3
- tall clean × 3

**Hang Clean — 5×2 @ 35–40 kg**

**Full Clean — 5–6×1 @ 40–45 kg**  
Touch 47.5–50 kg only when earlier reps are clean.

**Split-Jerk Footwork — 3×5**

**Split Jerk From Rack — 5×2 @ ~30–41 kg**

**Clean Pull — 4×3 @ 60–65 kg**  
Progress gradually toward 70–80+ kg.

**Bench Press — 3×5 @ ~60–67.5 kg**  
Maintenance / slow progression.

## Day C — Integration

**Power Snatch — 4×2 @ 25–32.5 kg**

**Power Clean + Split Jerk — 5×(1+1) @ 35–40 kg**

**Full Clean — 4×1 @ 40–47.5 kg**

**Back Squat — 3×5 @ 75–85 kg**

**RDL — 3×5 @ 70–80 kg**

**Row — 3×6–10**

# Scheduling

## Preferred: 3 days/week

- Day A
- Day B
- Day C

Keep at least one recovery day between the harder sessions when practical.

## If training 2 days/week

Rotate continuously:

- Week 1: A / B
- Week 2: C / A
- Week 3: B / C
- repeat

Do not permanently remove Day C.

# 8-week progression

## Weeks 1–3 — Skill acquisition

Week 1:
- snatch ~25–35 kg
- clean ~35–45 kg
- jerk ~20–35 kg

Week 2:
- repeat the same structure;
- add 2.5 kg only where technique remains crisp.

Week 3:
- snatch ~30–37.5 kg
- clean ~40–47.5 kg
- jerk ~30–40 kg

## Week 4 — Technical-max checkpoint

Work upward to the heaviest rep that remains technically strong.

Record:
- technical snatch max;
- technical clean max;
- technical clean & jerk max;
- technical split jerk max.

## Weeks 5–7 — Build from technical max

Use Week-4 technical maxes as anchors:

- Week 5: mostly 70–80%
- Week 6: mostly 75–85%
- Week 7: mostly 80–90%, low rep counts

Misses should stay rare.

## Week 8 — Taper + technical-max test

Reduce volume, then retest technical maxes.

The block succeeds if the same weights move faster, stay closer to the body, are received more confidently, require less conscious effort, and produce fewer ugly reps.

# Jerk strategy

Use the **split jerk as the primary heavy jerk** because it gives more recovery tolerance and a larger stable base.

Keep the **squat jerk as a secondary technical variation** if you enjoy it. It is legitimate but considerably less forgiving.

# Conditioning

During the initial learning block, prioritize movement quality over hard conditioning.

Prefer:
- walking;
- easy running;
- Zone 2.

Keep hard HIIT modest, particularly near important Olympic-lifting sessions.

# What to log

For every Olympic-lift working set, record:

- date;
- week;
- session;
- exercise;
- load;
- reps;
- technique score;
- RPE;
- made/missed;
- technical issue;
- cue or note.

Useful issue labels include:

- bar forward;
- early arm bend;
- hips rise too fast;
- jumping forward;
- slow turnover;
- bar crash;
- unstable catch;
- press-out;
- incomplete extension;
- jerk dip forward;
- short/unstable split.

The accompanying Excel workbook includes a session log, technical-max tracker, 8-week progression, your current strength baselines, and an auto-updating dashboard.

# First milestone

> **50 kg clean becomes technically boring.**

Once that happens, 55 kg can move from “ugly max” toward normal training weight, and 60+ kg becomes a much more sensible next target.
